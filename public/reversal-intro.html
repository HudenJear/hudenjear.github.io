Color Reversal Films Introduction

A practical overview of slide film: what it is, how to shoot it, and how to get the look you want.

What is color reversal film?

Color reversal film (slide film) produces a positive image directly on the film base. After E-6 processing, the result can be viewed on a light table or projected. Compared with color negative film, slide film typically has higher contrast and more limited exposure latitude.

Why shoot slide film?

Direct positive image

The film itself is the final color reference. Great for studying color and light.

Distinct color & contrast

Many stocks have a signature palette (Velvia, Provia, Ektachrome).

Scanning & archiving

Dense highlights can be hard—good scanning workflow matters.

Exposure tips (quick)

Protect highlights

Slide film holds highlights less than negative film. Avoid overexposure.

Meter for the scene

Use incident metering when possible; otherwise watch bright specular areas.

Consistent lab / process

E-6 consistency varies. Stick to a lab you trust for stable results.
